# M3 — Scripted expert (pick → handoff → insertion) ◐ → M3a

Status: **pick/transport/handoff stable (50/50)**; insertion under active work in **M3a**.

The scripted expert is the rule-based arm of the B-DASH hierarchy (brief §3.3) and the stand-in for
the VLA front-end (VLA training is out of scope). It drives the Franka through
`approach → descend → grasp → lift → transport → handoff`, then a rule-based controller does the
peg-in-hole insertion. All thresholds/gains live in `configs/bdash/peg_insert/controllers.yaml`.

root cause a rim-impact spike (~35 N) that pivoted the peg in the gripper (5–7° tilt) into a jam.

## M3a — §2.1-compliant insertion + contact-dynamics fix

Brief v2 raises the bar to **L1/c=2.0 ≥ 90% (50 trials)** *and* single-digit-N typical contact
force, and (§2.1) forbids feeding the **peg's ground-truth pose** into the insertion controller. The
v1 controller violated this (it drove off `peg.root_pos_w` / `root_quat_w`).

**Compliance refactor.** The controller now uses only:
- **socket pose** — allowed (jig-fixed, known fixture);
- **EE proprioception** — `tip_estimate(env, grip_offset)` = `ee_pos + R_ee·[0,0,grip_offset]`. The
  pick grasps the EE frame exactly `grip_offset` above the tip, so this recovers the tip from the
  `ee_frame` sensor + the nominal grasp constant. The residual (`tip_est ≠ true tip`, from peg
  slip/tilt in the gripper) is the realistic unobserved disturbance the F/T loop must absorb;
- **wrist-F/T model** — `_wrench()` = world-frame resultant of the peg contact sensor; `Fz` is the
  press load, `Fxy` the lateral binding/offset force. (The peg pose is still read by the predicates /
  §3.5 logger, which §2.1 explicitly permits.)

The brief's four measures are applied **one at a time** (`use_settle` / `use_force_servo` /
`rcc_*_gain` flags in `controllers.yaml`), each measured at L1/c=2.0 / 50 trials / seed=0, to keep
causal attribution (brief §3).

### Acceptance — MET ✅

**Final config** (`use_settle: true`, `use_force_servo: true`, RCC off): **L1/c=2.0 → 50/50 (100%)
≥ 90%**, typical insertion contact force **median 4.25 N / mean 5.6 N — single-digit** (brief §3 M3a
bar). `handoff` fires 50/50, geometry logged for E4/M7.

Headline finding: the success bar was solved by the **§2.1 compliance refactor itself** (peg-pose
feedback was causing tilt-chasing retry thrash → timeouts); the contact-force bar was solved by the
**low-force servo**, and the ~35 N `force_max_N` was a measurement artifact (grasp clamp), with the
true insertion press already single-digit. Settle is kept for the deterministic `handoff` log.

Also added (diff #2): failures that reach handoff but never seat are now logged as `insertion_failed`
(not `timeout`) — confirmed at c=0.25 where all 24 fails categorize as `insertion_failed`.

## M3b — SDF force-spike physics fix

Capped the **peg** `max_depenetration_velocity` 5.0 → **1.0 m/s** via a bdash-local deepcopy in
`bdash_peg_assets.py` (the shared `RIGID_BODY_PROPS_HIGH_PRECISION` is left untouched, §2.2). §2.3
stability re-check recorded in `docs/physics_params.md §7`.

**Result:** no c=2.0 regression (**50/50**); at the tight/misaligned c=0.25 case, capped vs uncapped
are statistically identical and **neither explodes** (force_max ~36 N, no NaN). The M1 SDF-spike risk
does **not** trigger in the funnel-guided scripted flow; the cap is kept as insurance for M7's
direct-placement harness (controlled offsets/tilts) — re-verify there. **Acceptance met** (no
explosion, no regression).

**Why M3a-0 alone fixed the success rate.** The v1 controller read the *true* peg pose, so when the
peg tilted in the gripper the lateral/tilt signals moved and it chased them into retries/tilt-aborts
that timed out. The proprioceptive controller commands the EE straight down on-axis and lets the
funnel mechanically self-correct — it does not chase the (now-unobserved) peg tilt, so it does not
thrash. This is both more realistic (real hardware has only proprioception + F/T) and more robust.

**Remaining M3a goal:** drive the contact force from ~35 N to single digits (settle + low-force
servo), and make `handoff` fire 50/50 (settle). These do not need to raise success (already 100%).

## Bug lessons
- **§2.1 compliance was also a robustness win.** Removing the privileged peg-pose feedback (required
  for compliance) eliminated the tilt-chasing retry thrash that caused the v1 timeouts. Reading less
  ground truth made the controller *better*, not worse.
