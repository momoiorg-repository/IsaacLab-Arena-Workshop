# M10 — VLA fine-tuning: the frozen-frames breakthrough ✅

Status: **first working closed-loop VLA.** After four model generations stuck at **0% success**, the
v6-recovery GR00T N1.7 model reaches **20% end-to-end (pick → handoff → insert)** at L1/c2.0 — driven
by **85% grasp** and **75% clean handoff**. The headline finding is not the model: it is the discovery
that *every prior 0% was an artifact of a silent camera bug* — the VLA had never actually seen the
peg move.

## 1. The task

V-DASH is a Franka peg-in-hole task: pick a peg off the table and insert it into a socket with **2 mm
radial clearance** at randomization level **L1** (peg + socket positions vary ~18 cm across the
workspace). The policy is a **hierarchy**: a fine-tuned **GR00T N1.7** vision-language-action model
runs the closed-loop **pick → handoff** (peg lifted and brought upright over the socket), then a
**rule-based `InsertionController`** (socket pose + wrist force/torque) performs the final insertion.
The VLA's only job — and the only thing being learned — is perception + grasp + handoff. Training data
is the **scripted expert's successful episodes** (no teleop), recorded and fine-tuned offline.

## 2. The long 0% (what didn't work, and why it was misleading)

Four model generations all evaluated at **0/20 success**, which sent us chasing the wrong causes:

| Gen | Change | Grasp | Success | Read at the time |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| v3 | square peg + yaw-aligned grasp | 0/20 | 0/20 | "yaw perception unlearnable from 200 demos" |
| v4 | cylinder peg + closer start + **N1.7** | 2/20 | 0/20 | "directionally right, needs grasp precision" |
| v4-28000 | same data, trained longer | 1/20 | 0/20 | "more steps don't help → it's the data" |
| v5-vision | unfreeze the vision encoder | 0/20 | 0/20 | "vision-tuning overfits 200 demos → regressed" |

A diagnostic (logging gripper-vs-peg geometry) showed the real symptom across **all** of them: the
gripper descended and closed at a **near-fixed point** — the *mean* of the peg's spawn distribution —
**12–14 cm** from the actual peg, regardless of where the peg was. The action worked; the fingers
closed fully. It was simply closing over empty space. That looks exactly like a perception failure, so
the hypotheses were all perception-flavoured: cameras too far, encoder frozen, not enough data.

## 3. Root cause: the cameras were frozen

The user noticed every frame in a recorded episode looked **identical**. Verified: in the recorded
HDF5 the robot moves fully (end-effector travels 8×13×22 cm, grasps the peg, reaches handoff) but the
three camera streams contained **exactly one unique frame** for the entire episode. The VLA had been
training on a **single static reset image + proprioception** — it had never seen the peg being
approached, so the best it could possibly learn was the open-loop mean trajectory. This invalidated
**all** prior datasets and eval results.

The bug is environment-specific and silent:

> On this box (**RTX 5080 / Blackwell, Isaac Sim 5.1, headless**) a standalone `sim.render()` does
> **not** refresh the RTX camera sensors — and that is exactly what `ManagerBasedRLEnv.step()`'s
> decimation loop uses. Only a render-**coupled** physics step (`sim.step(render=True)`) refreshes
> them. Reset frames *do* update (a different render path), which is why each episode had one correct
> frame and then froze.

Ruled out as causes (each tested): the recorder, `inference_mode`, `unset DISPLAY`, Fabric on/off,
tiled vs non-tiled cameras, render mode, and single-vs-multiple `render()` pumps.

## 4. The fix

After every `env.step`, pump one render-coupled step and recompute the observation, on **both** the
recording and the evaluation paths so train/eval stay consistent:

```python
# record_vla_demos.py  and  evaluation/policy_runner.py (gated on uenv.sim.has_rtx_sensors())
env.sim.step(render=True)
env.scene.update(env.physics_dt)
env.obs_buf = env.observation_manager.compute()   # next pre-step capture / policy input is now live
```

A secondary perception lever was folded in at the same time: the side cameras were **too far** (peg
was a ~3 px speck), so their focal length was raised 2.8 → 8.0 (peg → ~12–15 px), verified to keep the
worst-case L1 corner framed.

## 5. The dataset and model

- **Dataset** `umegan/vdash-pick-handoff-v5-recovery` — **1000 demos** re-recorded with the camera fix
  (verified: per-step frame motion, all frames unique). Composition: 500 clean + 250 wide-start
  (`arm_init_std` 0.15) + 250 **recovery** (a mid-trajectory joint kick that the scripted expert
  auto-recovers from — covariate-shift / DAgger-style robustness). 112,820 steps total.
- **Model** `umegan/vdash-gr00t-n1-7-pick-handoff-v6-recovery` — GR00T N1.7 (3B, Cosmos-Reason2-2B /
  Qwen3-VL backbone), **frozen vision encoder** (unfreezing regressed at this data scale), projector +
  diffusion heads tuned, ~2–3 epochs. Trained on cloud A100/H100 (the 3B fp32 fine-tune OOMs the local
  16 GB 5080; inference fits fine at ~20 step/s).

## 6. Result and where the bottleneck moved

At L1/c2.0, n=20: **17 grasp → 15 clean handoff → 4 insert.** The debug geometry confirms the VLA now
**localizes and tracks the peg to ~5 mm** (`dxy` 0.005–0.006) — the exact inverse of the prior
regression-to-mean. This empirically proves the frozen-frames bug was the true root cause of the
universal 0%, and it is the first eval that runtime-validates the eval-path camera fix (the model could
only perceive the peg because it was finally being fed live frames).

The bottleneck has **moved from VLA perception to the insertion phase**. Of 15 clean handoffs, only 4
convert (result breakdown: 4 success / 11 insertion-failed / 5 timeout). The insertion failures end
**canted** in the tight 2 mm hole (final tilt: mean 32°, median 19°, max 83°; contact 40–43 N). The VLA
handoff geometry (from the JSONL, n=15): tilt **mean 5.6° / median 4.9° / max 15.7°**, lateral \|dxy\|
**median 6.5 mm**, dx median **−0.8 mm**.

> **Superseded by M11 — read it for the corrected conclusion.** This section originally attributed the
> loss to the `InsertionController` "jamming." Isolation in M11 **disproves** that: the controller
> inserts a *clean* handoff at **98%** (and is *more* robust to handoff pose than assumed). The real
> limiter is **grasp quality** — the VLA holds the peg cocked **in the gripper** (~8° / ~9 mm), which a
> §2.1 peg-pose-blind controller cannot see or correct. (Earlier drafts of this section also cited
> "dx ≈ −5.7 mm, tilt ≈ 3.7°"; those were the EE→peg *tracking* distance from the debug stream, not the
> handoff error — corrected above.)

## 8. Lessons

- **A silent rendering no-op can masquerade as a model/data problem for weeks.** Four "perception"
  hypotheses (camera distance, vision-tuning, data volume) were all plausible and all wrong; the
  policy was simply never shown motion. *Verify the data the model actually receives before iterating
  on the model.* A one-line frame-uniqueness check would have caught it immediately.
- **Train/eval must share the fix.** The same frozen-frame bug lives in the eval stepping loop, so the
  recording fix alone would still evaluate a good model on frozen frames. Both paths were corrected and
  the gate (`has_rtx_sensors`) leaves non-camera evals untouched.
- **Fixing the bottleneck reveals the next one.** Perception solved → the limiter is in the insertion
  phase. **M11 localizes it precisely**: not the controller (98% from a clean handoff) but VLA **grasp
  quality** — a §2.1 peg-blind controller cannot correct a peg cocked in the gripper.
